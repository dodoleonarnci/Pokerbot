How these numbers are worked out
Spot
Street, heads-up or multiway, the bettor's role (raiser or caller before the flop, or what they did on the last street), whether they lead or bet after a check, and the bet's size as a share of the pot.
Folds too often
A bet of size s (as a share of the pot) with no hand breaks even when everyone folds s / (1 + s) of the time, a third for a half-pot bet. Above that, the profit per spot is the fold rate minus s times the call-or-raise rate, in pots.
Bluffs too often or too rarely
On the river a bet of size s is balanced when s / (1 + 2s) of the bets are bluffs, a quarter for a half-pot bet. A called river bet always reaches showdown, so those hands show the whole betting range. "Bluff" here means no pair made with their own cards. The value is what a hand that beats every bluff and loses to every value bet gains by calling (too many bluffs) or by folding (too few).
EM estimate
On the flop and turn, most bets never reach showdown. The estimate fits how opponents bet with each kind of hand, using every hand they played and each one's exposed card, and works out each bettor's most likely hidden card.
What's left out
Preflop spots, spots seen fewer than 30 times (10 for river showdowns), and our own bot's decisions. Folding bluff-catchers is valued as if we would always have called there, so that figure is an upper bound.
